# Communication Basics

Professional communication means sharing information clearly and respectfully while listening to others. It includes speaking, writing, asking questions, and noticing whether the audience understands. Good communication helps teams coordinate work, resolve misunderstandings, and make decisions. The right approach depends on the audience: a short chat message, a detailed report, and a project presentation need different levels of context.

## Organize a clear answer

Start with the main point, then explain the reason and give a supporting example. A simple pattern is point, reason, example, and takeaway. For instance, when describing a project choice, state the decision, explain the constraint, show what happened, and mention the result. Define unfamiliar terms before using them. Short sentences and clear transitions help listeners follow an explanation.

## Listening and questions

Active listening means paying attention to the speaker, checking your understanding, and responding to what they actually said. You can summarize a point in your own words or ask a focused follow-up question. If an instruction is unclear, clarify the goal, deadline, and expected format. This is more useful than guessing and discovering later that the work solved a different problem.

## Feedback and disagreement

Constructive feedback describes an observable action and its effect, then offers a useful next step. For example: “The report is missing the test results, so reviewers cannot verify the change. Could you add them before the release review?” When you disagree, acknowledge the shared goal, explain your evidence, and invite discussion. Respectful disagreement can improve a decision without making the conversation personal.

## The STAR method

STAR stands for Situation, Task, Action, and Result. It is a structure for answering experience-based interview questions. Briefly describe the context, state your responsibility, focus on the steps you took, and finish with the outcome or lesson. For a teamwork example, explain the shared goal, your contribution, how you coordinated, and what the team achieved. Keep the Action section specific so the listener can understand your choices.

## Key points

- Lead with the main point and support it with evidence.
- Adapt detail and tone to the audience and situation.
- Listen actively and ask focused questions when needed.
- Give feedback about actions and effects, not personal traits.
- Use STAR to tell concise, truthful stories about past work.
